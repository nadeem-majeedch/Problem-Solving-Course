# Assessment Plan

The course rewards *demonstrated reasoning*. No component requires memorising
solutions; quizzes and assignments always present problems in the same style
as lecture cases but not copied from them.

## Components and weighting

| Component | Weight | Outcomes | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Case participation (in-class attempts) | 10% | all | every lecture |
| Quizzes (8, best 6 count) | 20% | LO2–LO10 | see outline |
| Labs (8 of 12, completion-graded) | 15% | LO1–LO10 (see matrix) | Blocks I–IV |
| Assignments (5 of 6; Assignment 6 may replace the lowest) | 25% | LO1–LO10 | see outline |
| Term project (A, B, or approved proposal) | 30% | LO1, LO4, LO6, LO7, LO9, LO10 | last four weeks |

Institutions with different credit structures may re-weight; keep the
participation component small and the project substantial.

## Case participation (10%)

Each lecture, students submit their attempt for one designated case (marked
*submit today* in the lecture plan) as pseudocode, flowchart, or Python.
Grading is completion-based with brief written feedback: full credit for a
serious attempt that shows decomposition, regardless of correctness. The
lowest three lectures are dropped to accommodate absences.

## Exit questions (ungraded, diagnostic)

Every lecture plan's *Formative assessment* section ends with three exit
questions and their expected answers. Students answer one on a card in the
closing minute. These are not graded: the instructor skims them before the
next lecture and uses them to pick the framing, the review-station topics,
and the quiz distractors. Persistent wrong answers route individual students
to office hours — the earliest intervention signal the course has.

## Quizzes (20%)

Eight 20-minute quizzes, given in the first half-hour of the designated
lecture (see [Course Outline](course-outline.md)). Each quiz has 3–4 short
cases in the lecture-case style plus one trace-the-code item; the mix covers
concepts, tracing, debugging, pseudocode, complexity, and reasoning
(per-quiz coverage in the outline). Answer keys are in `quizzes/keys/`
(instructor distribution only). Best six of eight count.

## Labs (15%)

Twelve guided, machine-based sessions follow the corresponding lectures
(see the [course outline](course-outline.md) calendar); each is a 2-hour
paired session whose submission is a short report answering the lab's
*checkpoints*. Labs are completion-graded against the checkpoint guidance
in each lab page. The best eight of twelve count; instructors schedule
the twelve from the outline's calendar slots and drop the rest.

## Term project (30%)

Students choose one project from [Projects](../projects/) —
[Project A](../projects/project-a.md) (canteen data mini-study),
[Project B](../projects/project-b.md) (campus route planner),
[Project C](../projects/project-c.md) (campus network explorer, first-
semester friendly) — or propose one with the
[proposal template](../projects/proposal-template.md).
Deliverables: a decomposition document, a working program, a test table, and a
short analysis section (efficiency + one data-oriented question). The rubric
is in [projects/rubric.md](../projects/project-rubric.md); the midpoint
conversation uses the [progress review checklist](../projects/progress-checklist.md).
First-semester students take Project C; second-semester students may take
Project A; third-semester students should attempt Project B or an
approved proposal.

## Grade bands

| Band | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Excellent | ≥ 85% with project rubric "advanced" on reasoning and testing |
| Good | 70–84% |
| Satisfactory | 55–69% |
| Fail | < 55% or fewer than 4 participation submissions |

## Integrity

Discussing approaches is encouraged; submitting another student's attempt,
code, or write-up is not. Quizzes are closed-book; assignments and the project
may use any *course* material. If external help is used, students must state
it in one line at the top of the submission. Instructors should apply their
institution's policy where it is stricter.

## Feedback loop

- Quizzes returned at the next lecture with the three most common errors shown (anonymously).
- Assignments returned within one week with rubric marks plus one strength and one fix.
- Project: midpoint check (decomposition document) counts 5 of the 30 points.
