# Project A — The Canteen Data Mini-Study

**Individual or pairs (second semester appropriate) · Data you generate.**

## The brief

The canteen committee wants to know how students actually use it, but has
no data. You will *simulate* three weeks of canteen sales with a
documented generator, then analyse your own data as if it were real —
including all the honesty problems that creates.

## Part 1 — The generator (program)

Write `generate.py` producing `sales.csv` with columns
`day, item, price, count`:

- 15 weekdays (three weeks), 4 items with fixed prices you choose;
- each day's counts random within item-specific ranges (e.g. sandwich
  20–60, soup 10–40 …), with one documented weekday effect of your choice
  (e.g. Fridays sell 30% fewer hot meals);
- a fixed random seed so your data is exactly reproducible;
- deliberate imperfections, each occurring at low, *stated* rates:
  occasional negative counts, one duplicated day per week, and rare
  off-menu items.

## Part 2 — Clean and profile (program + report)

Write `clean.py` that reports (not silently fixes) every defect it finds:
rows dropped, rows fixed, duplicates merged — each with a count and a
rule. Produce a one-page profile: total sales, best day, best item, and
the distribution of daily totals.

## Part 3 — Answer three questions (report)

1. Which item earns the most across the three weeks, and does the weekday
   effect survive the missing/duplicate handling?
2. Is the "best day" stable if you re-run the generator with a different
   seed? (Run 5 seeds; report the spread.)
3. What question about the canteen can this data *not* answer, and why?

## Analysis section

- Efficiency: what grows with the number of rows; show the arithmetic.
- The named design decision the course did not make for you (e.g. your
  weekday-effect representation, your duplicate-merge rule).
- Limitations: what the simulation assumes that real canteens violate.

## Data rules

- Everything is generated by you; the generator is part of the submission.
- Seed reproducibility is mandatory: graders re-run your generator.
- Every cleaning rule is stated in the report — silent deletion fails the
  integrity bar for this project.
