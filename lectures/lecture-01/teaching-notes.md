# Teaching Notes — Lecture 01

*Instructor only - not rendered on the public site.*

## Board plan

- Left: the three-part frame - INPUTS / OUTPUTS / RULE - stays visible all hour.
- Middle: the coffee card traced as a stamp table (drink, stamps, paid/free).
- Right: 'assumptions we made today' - grows as each reading decision appears.

## Questions to ask students

- What information did the problem give us that we did NOT need?
- Where exactly did the two readings of the rule diverge - at drink 9 or drink 10?
- Which assumption, if flipped, changes the answer by the most money?

## Alternative explanations

- For students frozen by open-endedness: give the frame as a fill-in sheet (inputs ____ / outputs ____ / rule ____).
- For the experienced: ask for the closed form (paid = wanted - wanted // 10) and when it beats the loop.

## Expected student difficulties

- First lecture: many will wait for 'the right answer' - celebrate any stated assumption as the day's win.
- Mixed experience shows immediately: pair experienced with newcomers for the case attempts.

## Connections to neighbouring lectures

Sets the course contract: every lecture opens with a case, attempts precede reveals. Next: turning the coffee-card reasoning into punctilious pseudocode (L2).

## Quiz answer key

**A1.** Name the three parts of the problem frame used in every case.
- *Expected:* Inputs, outputs, rule.

**A2.** What is an assumption in this course?
- *Expected:* A stated choice about unclear wording or missing information, made before solving.

**A3.** Give one reason two correct students can get different answers.
- *Expected:* They made different (both legitimate) readings of an ambiguous rule.

**A4.** What belongs in a problem's edge cases list?
- *Expected:* Empty/zero inputs, boundaries of ranges, and the maximum expected size.

**B1.** Frame the coffee card (buy 9, get the 10th free, 3.50 per paid drink, 22 drinks): inputs, outputs, rule, and ONE assumption you must state.
- *What earns marks:* Inputs: price, wanted count, card rule. Outputs: paid count, free count, total. Rule: every 9 paid drinks earn one free. Assumption: free drinks do earn a stamp (or not - state which).

**B2.** A library fine is 0.50 per day up to 7 days, then 1.00 per day. List the inputs, outputs, and two ambiguities you would resolve with the librarian.
- *What earns marks:* Inputs: days late, fee schedule. Outputs: fine. Ambiguities: does 'up to 7' include day 7; is the count calendar days or open days.

## Exit ticket - expected answers

1. State the four questions you must answer before attempting any case.
   - *Expected:* inputs, outputs, constraints, assumptions (edge cases named from them)

2. Give one example of two defensible readings of the same rule.
   - *Expected:* e.g. 'every 6th drink free' — counting only paid drinks vs all drinks

3. Why is the five-minute attempt not wasted time?
   - *Expected:* it generates the class's own attempts, making the reveal answer a question already asked
