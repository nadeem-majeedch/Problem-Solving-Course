# Activity — Sketch That Flow

**Format for early lectures (01–04) and the recursion lecture (17) · 7 minutes.**

1. Project a short problem statement *with no numbers in it* (e.g.
   "decide whether a purchased bundle is cheaper than its parts").
2. Students sketch a flowchart in 4 minutes using only: start/end,
   decision diamonds, input/output boxes, arrows. No code, no
   pseudocode — the constraint is the point.
3. Swap sketches; the reviewer finds one path where the arrows disagree
   with the words. 2 minutes.
4. Instructor projects a clean version; class traces one input through it.

**Why it works.** Students who hide behind half-remembered syntax are
forced to expose their control flow. It is also the quickest diagnostic
for the classic "decision diamond with no exit" mistake.

**Pitfall.** Cap the problem at one loop and two decisions. Anything more
and the sketches become art criticism instead of logic review.
