# Assignment 6 — The Method Portfolio (Block IV)

**Due: week 31 · Weight: see [assessment plan](../docs/assessment-plan.md) ·
Covers LO1, LO6, LO10, LO12 · Submit a written portfolio + one program.**

This assignment is the bridge to the term project: you will assemble —
and defend — the problem-solving method you have actually built across
the course, using the course's own case studies as your evidence base.

## Part 1 — The portfolio (8 marks)

Choose **three** case studies from *different blocks* (Block I: cs-001–016,
Block II: cs-017–032, Block III: cs-033–064, Block IV: cs-065–128 — any
three from distinct blocks; IDs from the
[case catalog](../case-studies/CATALOG.md)).

For each, one page:

1. **The method you would apply today** — not the case's published
   method, yours: name the first three moves you make on this problem
   and why each comes before the next. *(3)*
2. **What the case taught that generalises** — one sentence, and one
   counter-example: a *different* problem where the same move would be
   the wrong first move. *(3)*
3. **The trace** — re-solve the case's worked instance fresh (closed
   book; the point is that the method, not memory, does the work) and
   include your hand trace or the smallest program. *(2)*

## Part 2 — The adversarial review (4 marks)

Swap portfolios with a partner. For each of their three pages, write
one challenge: either a case where their stated first move leads them
astray, or a hole in their generalisation claim. Be concrete: name the
input or the move. Then write one sentence accepting or rebutting each
challenge — both are fine; unaddressed challenges are not.

## Part 3 — The unifying program (8 marks)

One program, ≤ 60 lines, standard library only, that demonstrates your
portfolio's through-line: it must contain (i) one loop whose invariant
you can state, (ii) one decomposition into ≥ 3 functions with stated
contracts, (iii) one test table with a deliberate-failure row, and
(iv) one paragraph naming which portfolio page each part exercises.
The *problem* it solves is your choice — a validator for the university's
email addresses, a small meeting-scheduler, a text-statistics tool —
but it must be yours, not a course case restated.

## Independent-reasoning component

Part 1's pages are inherently personal (your moves, your
counter-examples) and Part 2 is written *about* a specific partner's
portfolio — both are plagiarism-resistant by construction; submitting a
portfolio whose pages could belong to anyone forfeits the mark. State
your Part 2 partner by name. See [integrity](../docs/assessment-plan.md).

## Submission checklist

- Three portfolio pages (≤ 1 page each), block labels visible.
- The Part 2 challenges with accept/rebut sentences, partner named.
- The Part 3 program with its test table and mapping paragraph.
- Length guide: 4–6 pages total.
