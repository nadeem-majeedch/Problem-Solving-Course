Key definitions

Key definitions

  • Mean — the total divided by the count; sensitive to every value, especially extremes
  • Median — the middle value of the sorted data; robust to extremes
  • Skew — asymmetry in the data's shape; the mean-median gap is its alarm
  • Percentage points — the arithmetic difference of two percentages
  • Relative change — the difference divided by the starting value
  • Honest summary — a claim plus its denominator, its n, and the summary choice it depends on
Explain each in one breath; the cases will do the heavy lifting.
Worked example

The Salary Parable: mean vs median under skew

cs-109

Salaries 30k, 32k, 35k, 38k, 200k. Which summary survives, and how would each be (mis)used?

sal = [30, 32, 35, 38, 200]
mean = sum(sal) / len(sal)
sorted_sal = sorted(sal)
median = sorted_sal[len(sorted_sal) // 2]
print(mean, median)

Expected: mean 67k, median 35k - the 32k gap is the skew alarm

Cost: O(n) mean, O(n log n) median by sorting (O(n) selection exists - enrichment).

Worked example

The Percentage Maze: percentages of what?

cs-110

A price rises 20%, then falls 20%. Back to the start? And: churn fell from 10% to 8% - is that '2%' or '20%' better?

price = 100 * 1.20 * 0.80
points = 10 - 8
relative = (10 - 8) / 10
print(price, points, relative)

Expected: price ends at 96 (not 100); churn: -2 points = -20% relative

Cost: O(1); the skill is vocabulary discipline, not arithmetic.

Worked example

The Waiting-Time Twist: averages that hide the experience

cs-111

Waiting times (minutes): 684 customers' experiences compress to: most wait 2, a few wait 60+. Report honestly: which average, and what must accompany it?

waits = [2, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5, 60, 70]
med = sorted(waits)[len(waits) // 2]
mean = sum(waits) / len(waits)
tail = sum(1 for w in waits if w > 30) / len(waits)
print(med, round(mean, 1), tail)

Expected: median 3 min, mean 18.5 min, 25% wait over 30 min

Cost: O(n log n) with the sort; all three summaries from one pass over sorted data.

Check yourself

Common misconceptions

  • Quoting the mean of skewed data as 'the average' without the median beside it.
  • Swapping percentage points and percent - churn 'down 2 points' is 'down 20 percent', not the same claim.
  • Adding percentage changes across shifting bases - up 20% then down 20% is a net 4% loss.
  • Believing a chart cannot lie if the numbers are true - truncated axes and chosen baselines mislead.
