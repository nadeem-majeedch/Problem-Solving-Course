Key definitions

Key definitions

  • Record — one row of a table: the values describing one entity (one student, one sale)
  • Grouping — splitting records into buckets by a shared field before aggregating
  • Aggregation — collapsing many values into one summary: total, average, maximum, count
  • Rate vs count — a rate divides by its base (per person, per day); a count does not
  • Pivot — a two-way grouping: rows for one field, columns for another, cells holding aggregates
  • Denominator — the 'per what' of a rate; the first thing a reader must know
Explain each in one breath; the cases will do the heavy lifting.
Worked example

The Canteen Queue Log: from log to ledger

cs-097

Log lines like '0812 ada lunch 6.50', '0815 bo snack 2.00', '0809 ada lunch 4.00'. Build per-person totals and the busiest item - decide your cleaning rules first.

lines = ["0812 ada lunch 6.50", "0815 bo snack 2.00", "0809 ada lunch 4.00"]
totals, items = {}, {}
for ln in lines:
    _t, person, item, amt = ln.split()
    totals[person] = totals.get(person, 0.0) + float(amt)
    items[item] = items.get(item, 0) + 1
busiest = sorted(items.items(), key=lambda kv: (-kv[1], kv[0]))[0][0]
print(round(totals["ada"], 2), busiest)

Expected: ada total 10.50; busiest item 'lunch' (2 visits)

Cost: O(n) for n log lines; the ranking is O(u log u) over distinct items.

Worked example

Screen Time by Cohort: group, then compare honestly

cs-098

(cohort, minutes) pairs: (A, 120), (B, 90), (A, 180), (B, 110), (A, 60). Compare mean screen time per cohort - and state what the comparison cannot tell you.

rows = [("A", 120), ("B", 90), ("A", 180), ("B", 110), ("A", 60)]
groups = {}
for c, m in rows:
    groups.setdefault(c, []).append(m)
for c in sorted(groups):
    v = groups[c]
    print(c, len(v), sum(v) / len(v))

Expected: A: mean 120 (n=3); B: mean 100 (n=2)

Cost: O(n) grouping, O(g) reporting - trivial cost, the care is free but must be spent.

Worked example

The Churn Story: pivot and percentage, denominator stated

cs-099

Cohorts: A: 40 stayed, 10 left; B: 25 stayed, 25 left. Which cohort has the worse churn rate, and what exactly does the percentage measure?

cohorts = {"A": (40, 10), "B": (25, 25)}
for c, (stayed, left) in sorted(cohorts.items()):
    total = stayed + left
    print(c, f"{left / total:.0%}", total)

Expected: A 20% churn, B 50% churn (denominator: cohort members)

Cost: O(1) per cohort; the intellectual work is in the denominator, not the arithmetic.

Check yourself

Common misconceptions

  • Reporting rates without denominators - '20%' of what population?
  • Averaging averages - groups of different sizes need weighting, not naive averaging.
  • Rounding during accumulation - round only at presentation.
  • Reading causation from a grouped comparison - the pivot shows association, never mechanism.
