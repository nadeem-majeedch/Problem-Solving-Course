# Quiz 8 — Capstone (Lectures 29–32)

**Time: 20 minutes · Closed book · Answer all questions.**
This quiz samples the whole course; each question names the lecture that
taught the tool.

## Q1. Formulation (5 marks)

A student council has 300 and wants to fund as many events as possible.
Available events have costs 40, 55, 80, 120, 150, and 190; each event is
either funded in full or not at all.

(a) State the objective and the constraint in one sentence each. *(2)*
(b) Give the optimal selection and its objective value. *(2)*
(c) Which lecture's technique solves this at general scale, and why does
    "cheapest first" work here? *(1)*

## Q2. Expected value (5 marks)

A raffle ticket costs 2. Prizes: one prize of 50 with probability 1/200,
ten prizes of 5 with probability 1/20 each (independent of the first
question — treat the two prize types as separate, non-overlapping draws).

(a) Compute the expected value of one ticket, showing the calculation. *(3)*
(b) Should a rational player buy, on expected value alone? One sentence. *(1)*
(c) Name one factor outside expected value that could still justify buying. *(1)*

## Q3. Strategy under uncertainty (5 marks)

A data-science team must choose: ship Model A (accurate on historical data,
untested on live traffic) or Model B (less accurate on history, but robust
in a one-week live pilot).

(a) Frame this as a decision under uncertainty: what are the outcomes and
    what is unknown? *(2)*
(b) The team can buy one week of live data about Model A before deciding.
    In one sentence, what is the value of that information? *(2)*
(c) Which lecture's framing is this? *(1)*

## Q4. The 20-minute final (5 marks)

You are handed: *"The canteen wants to predict which day next week will sell
the most sandwiches, using last month's sales."*

(a) Decompose into subproblems (list them). *(2)*
(b) State the two riskiest assumptions. *(2)*
(c) Name the first lecture's rule that (b) exercises. *(1)*
