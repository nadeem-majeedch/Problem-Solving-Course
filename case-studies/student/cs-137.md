## Problem

A lab server must back up 500 GB every night inside a one-hour window.
Two designs are on the table; all failure probabilities are **stated
assumptions about this lab's hardware**, not measurements:

- **Plan A — one run:** a single 50-minute job at 10 GB/min. Any night,
  the run fails with probability 1/10; a failed run wastes its full 50
  minutes (one attempt per night — the window cannot fit two).
- **Plan B — five chunks:** five sequential 10-minute jobs of 100 GB
  each. Each chunk fails independently with probability 1/50; a failed
  chunk wastes only its own 10 minutes and restarts alone.

Your deliverables:

1. For each plan: the probability of a **clean night** (no restarts).
2. For each plan: the **expected wasted minutes** per night.
3. Your recommendation with the one assumption whose truth the
   recommendation depends on most.
4. One reliability trade-off that the expected-value comparison does
   not capture.

If the problem is ambiguous anywhere, state the assumption you are making
and carry on — a stated assumption is part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** the two plans with their failure probabilities and costs
- **Output:** plan A: clean night 0.900, expected waste 5.0 min — plan
  B: clean night 0.904, expected waste 1.0 min — plus recommendation

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- "Expected failed runs" and "expected wasted time" are different
  quantities — a 50-minute failure and a 10-minute failure do not weigh
  the same, so multiply probability by the *cost* of the outcome.
- Plan B's clean-night probability is close to Plan A's, but its waste
  per failure is five times smaller — the two metrics disagree, and the
  decision must say which it is buying.
- Independence is an assumption, not a fact: a dying disk makes chunk
  failures correlated, and the whole comparison rests on that.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"The lab's disk is aging: chunk failures are no longer
independent (one failure makes the next twice as likely). Redo plan B's
clean-night probability under a simple correlation story of your choice
— stated explicitly — and say whether the recommendation survives."**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Advanced (expected-value reasoning with design stakes).
- Topics: risk, trade-offs, strategy.
- Restart costs are exactly the stated durations; bandwidth is constant.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a first attempt; partial answers with
  clear reasoning are worth more than silent struggling.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: for plan A, what is the expected *number of runs* per
  night, and what fraction of runs fail?
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: find the per-chunk failure probability p at which
  plan B's expected waste equals plan A's — and interpret the number.
