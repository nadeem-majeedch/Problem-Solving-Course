## Problem

Buses every 10 minutes, yet average wait is 15. Why your sample differs from the schedule's average (length-biased sampling), and the fix.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, the waiting-time summary recomputed correctly, and one hidden averaging choice that changes the story. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** buses every 10 min exactly; you arrive at a random minute
- **Output:** your expected wait 5 min if you believe the schedule; with bunching (headways 2,2,26 avg 10), rider average = (2^2+2^2+26^2)/(2*30) = 11.4 min

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- You are more likely to land in a LONG gap: rider-wait weights each gap by its length (inspection paradox).
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.
