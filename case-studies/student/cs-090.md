## Problem

Choose the most non-overlapping meetings. Sort by end time; argue the choice with an exchange argument. Greedy with a proof-shaped justification.

Your deliverable is the recurrence with base cases, the answer for the worked instance, and the memoised (or tabulated) version. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** meetings [(0,3), (1,2), (3,5), (4,7), (7,9)]
- **Output:** max non-overlapping = 4: (1,2), (3,5), (7,9) + one of (0,3)/(4,7) variant -> verify count 4

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Sort by END time; take a meeting whenever it starts at/after the last taken end. Justify by exchange argument.
- Trust the recursive call: if f(smaller) is correct, the transition is all you must get right.
- A DP table is the call tree, cached.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"State f(k)'s meaning in words; is your statement precise enough to code from?"**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Foundational (early-semester techniques assumed: precise statements, traces, simple loops).
- Topics: greedy, exchange argument.

- Time-box: about five minutes; naming the pattern and stating the assumption is

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: identify the pattern the worked instance uses before
  computing anything.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: Try the greedy rule, break it, then repair with dynamic programming.
