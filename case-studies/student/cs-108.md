## Problem

Temperature sensor with gaps and spikes. Distinguish dropout vs anomaly, impute or flag, and show how each choice changes a downstream average.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, the gap rule (missing vs zero vs delayed) made precise, and one record where the rule choice changes the conclusion. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** temps: [12, 12.5, 13, None, 47, 12.8, None, 13]
- **Output:** None = dropout (impute or flag, do NOT average over silently); 47 = spike (anomaly vs real heatwave - policy needed)

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Distinguish GAPS (no data) from OUTLIERS (unlikely data) - they get different treatments.
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Which assumption would you attack first if you had to argue the other side?"**
