## Problem

Count lineups of 5 students where two specific students refuse to stand together. Inclusion-exclusion in plain language first.

Your deliverable is the count (or probability) with the sample space stated, the case analysis, and a brute-force check for a small instance. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** 5 students; students P and Q refuse to stand together
- **Output:** 5! = 120; together (block): 4! x 2 = 48; apart: 120 - 48 = 72

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Count ALL, count BAD (glue P and Q into one block, 2 orders inside), subtract.
- 'How many total, how many favourable' is the whole skeleton of probability.
- Complements turn hairy unions into one subtraction.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Where exactly did the wrong counts double-count — point at the pair."**

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: identify the pattern the worked instance uses before
  computing anything.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: Re-derive the count with a different method and reconcile the answers.
