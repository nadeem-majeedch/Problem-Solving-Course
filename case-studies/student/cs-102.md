## Problem

If 4% of clicks are accidental and 10% of clicks are on ads, what's the range for both-at-once? Bounds without independence; exact value with it.

Your deliverable is the count (or probability) with the sample space stated, the case analysis, and a brute-force check for a small instance. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** P(accidental) = 0.04, P(ad click) = 0.10
- **Output:** P(both) is between max(0, 0.04+0.10-1)=0 and min(0.04, 0.10)=0.04; with independence: 0.004

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Without independence only BOUNDS are honest. State them, then state what independence would add.
- 'How many total, how many favourable' is the whole skeleton of probability.
- Complements turn hairy unions into one subtraction.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Where exactly did the wrong counts double-count — point at the pair."**
