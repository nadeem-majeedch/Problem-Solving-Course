## Problem

People stand in a circle; every k-th is removed until one remains. Derive the survivor with a recurrence, verify by simulation. A first taste of proof plus experiment.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, the survivor count for the worked instance, the counting rule in one sentence, and the wrap boundary (who is counted when the circle closes) traced. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** n = 7 people, k = 3
- **Output:** survivor position 4 (0-indexed 3)

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Recurrence on the circle size: J(1)=0; J(n) = (J(n-1)+k) mod n. Prove it by drawing n=5.
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.

## Discussion starter

prompt: **"Which assumption would you attack first if you had to argue the other side?"**
