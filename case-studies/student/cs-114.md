## Problem

One server, random arrivals: simulate waits under different arrival rates. See queueing behaviour emerge; locate the collapse point.

Your deliverable is the simulation result with trials and spread, the one-trial story, and the comparison with exact reasoning. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** arrivals Poisson-ish rate 4/hour, service 5/hour, simulate 400 hours
- **Output:** low waits at rho=0.8; collapse as rate approaches service capacity - queues are nonlinear

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Simulate minute by minute: arrival with prob rate/60, service finishes with prob srv/60. Plot (or tabulate) the average wait.
- A simulation is only as good as the story of one trial.
- Error shrinks like 1/sqrt(trials) — quadruple the work, halve the error.
