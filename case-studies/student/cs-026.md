## Problem

An invoice tool includes one extra row under specific conditions. Use bisection over input shapes to find the boundary condition that triggers the defect.

Your deliverable is the minimal failing input, the ranked hypotheses, the located mechanism, the minimal fix, and a regression test. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** invoice rows; extra row appears only when qty is exactly 10
- **Output:** boundary bug: condition qty >= 10 should be qty > 10; minimal repro: one row qty=10

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Bisect quantities: 9 fails? 10 fails? 11 fails? The edge between pass and fail IS the bug.
- A bug is a claim about your mental model; the failing input is the experiment.
- The fix should be smaller than the explanation of the bug.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Which hypothesis did you rank first, and what evidence moved it down the list?"**

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: identify the pattern the worked instance uses before
  computing anything.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: Design the smallest input that still exposes the defect you fixed.
