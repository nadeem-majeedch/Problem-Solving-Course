## Problem

Pick numbers from a list to hit an exact target sum. Full enumeration is 2^n; show the cost curve, solve small n, then sketch why cleverness is needed for big n.

Your deliverable is the enumerated answer, the space count, and the pruning rule with its measured effect. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** [3, 7, 1, 2, 9], target 12 -> 3+9 or 1+2+9... enumerate subsets
- **Output:** 2^5 = 32 subsets; subset-sum found: {3, 9} and {1, 2, 9}; cost curve doubles each +1 item

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Enumerate subsets via bit masks 0..2^n-1; compute the cost table for n = 10, 20, 30 to feel 2^n.
- Brute force is a legitimate algorithm when the space is honest about its size.
- Pruning is not cheating; it is arithmetic applied early.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Double the input size: is this still acceptable? Show the arithmetic."**
