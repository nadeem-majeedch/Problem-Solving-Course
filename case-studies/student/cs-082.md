## Problem

Versions 1..n, one onward is 'bad'. Find the first bad one with minimal checks. The canonical boundary-finding binary search.

Your deliverable is the located answer, the predicate, and the bound updates shown for one full trace. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** n = 12 versions, first bad = 8; is_bad calls counted
- **Output:** first bad = 8 found with 4 calls (vs 8 linear)

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Find FIRST bad, not 'a' bad: when test(mid) is true, answer is at mid or before -> hi = mid.
- Binary search is a loop plus a monotone question — nothing more.
- Half-open intervals keep the arithmetic honest.
