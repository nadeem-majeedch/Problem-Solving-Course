## Problem

The club attendance app shows **"0 attended"** for last week's session.
There were definitely people in the room. The session lead wants to email
everyone who "missed" it — tonight.

The app's data export contains these columns:
`badge_id, in_time, out_time, session, device`.

Your deliverable — **no code**:

1. At least three plausible meanings of "attended" that this export
   could support (each interpretation names the columns it needs).
2. The single clarifying question you would send to the app team first —
   the one whose answer changes the most.
3. A draft opening line for the email that does **not** accuse anyone of
   skipping, and stays honest no matter which interpretation turns out
   to be true.

If the problem is ambiguous anywhere, state the assumption you are making
and carry on — a stated assumption is part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** the export column list; the session lead's request
- **Output:** three interpretations with their required columns, one
  clarifying question, one safe email opening line

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- "0 attended" is a **claim about the world**, and data pipelines fail
  silently: an offline badge reader produces the same export as an empty
  room.
- The decision (an email to every "absent" student) is consequential and
  one-directional — this is exactly when asking beats coding.
- Each interpretation of "attended" is a different *rule*, and each rule
  names the columns that would let you compute it.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"The app team answers: 'the device was offline for that
session, but the door log has everyone.' Does that settle it? What do
you now owe the session lead before the email goes out?"**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Intermediate (requirements analysis under ambiguity).
- Topics: requirements, stakeholders, communication.
- You have no access to the app's internals — only the export columns
  listed and questions to the team.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a first attempt; partial answers with
  clear reasoning are worth more than silent struggling.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: what is the *cheapest* observation that distinguishes
  "empty room" from "broken pipeline"?
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: draft the two-line policy you would propose so
  this session lead never needs you to arbitrate "attended" again.
