## Problem

A corner shop has 20 shelves. One assistant visits each shelf **once
per day, in a fixed order**, and can restock only what fits in their
cart (capacity 30 units). Each shelf sells a known number of units per
day:

```
shelf:   A  B  C  D  E  F  G  H  I  J
per day: 9  5  7  3  8  2  6  4  5  6
```

(shelves K–T total 41 units/day more, same structure). A shelf must
**never** reach zero during the day: sales happen after the assistant's
visit. The cart is refilled to 30 units at the start of each day.

Your deliverables:

1. The restock rule the assistant follows each day (in words, then as
   two lines of pseudocode).
2. Worked check on shelf A: does your rule keep A stocked all day,
   every day, with daily visits? Show one day.
3. The failure case: which shelf goes empty first if visits drop to
   **every second day**, and why the rule cannot save it?
4. The trade-off sentence: capacity 30 vs daily cadence — which would
   you negotiate first, and what evidence for it?

If the problem is ambiguous anywhere, state the assumption you are making
and carry on — a stated assumption is part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** per-day sales rates, cart capacity 30, cadence 1 day
- **Output:** a rule of the form "restock shelf X to at least 2× its
  daily rate" — shelf A safe — and the identified failure shelf under
  cadence 2

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- "Never empty before the next visit" converts directly into a target:
  stock after a visit must cover sales until the *next* visit, with
  margin for the day's swing.
- The binding constraint is the pair (capacity, cadence): halving the
  cadence doubles the required per-visit stock, and some shelf's demand
  will exceed what a cart of 30 can carry.
- A rule that works for one shelf but needs the cart to carry 90 units
  is not a rule — check the total against capacity.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Management offers a second assistant who can start mid-day
but carries only 15 units. Model the effect on your rule's binding
constraint in two sentences — no full simulation needed."**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Intermediate (constraint reasoning across a small system).
- Topics: inventory, greedy, trade-offs.
- Sales are steady at the stated per-day rate; no spikes this week.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a first attempt; partial answers with
  clear reasoning are worth more than silent struggling.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: write shelf A's "stock at close" equation in terms of its
  restock amount.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: express the minimum cart capacity as a formula in
  the per-day rates and the cadence — then check it against the ten
  shelves given.
