## Problem

MCQ exam: guess vs leave blank with negative marking. Simulate strategies over many papers; find the break-even knowledge level.

Your deliverable is the simulation result with trials and spread, the one-trial story, and the comparison with exact reasoning. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** 40 questions, 4 options each; +1 correct, -0.25 wrong; guess-all vs leave-blank
- **Output:** guess-all EV = (1/4)(+1) + (3/4)(-0.25) = +0.0625 per question — the marking was calibrated so blind guessing is near-neutral; with partial knowledge (one option eliminated) EV = +0.1667 per question, so guessing wins once you can rule something out

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Compute EV per question for each strategy; the break-even is where elimination knowledge changes the sign.
- A simulation is only as good as the story of one trial.
- Error shrinks like 1/sqrt(trials) — quadruple the work, halve the error.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"How many trials would you demand before believing a 1-in-1000 event estimate?"**
