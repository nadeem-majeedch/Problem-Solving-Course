## Problem

Three small programs, each with a distinct classic defect class (scope, mutation, boundary). Localise all three under a time budget, then rank difficulty.

Your deliverable is the minimal failing input, the ranked hypotheses, the located mechanism, the minimal fix, and a regression test. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** three snippets: (a) counter defined outside a function, (b) shared default list, (c) range(len(xs)) deletion loop
- **Output:** (a) scope defect; (b) persistent-state defect; (c) skip-after-remove defect

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- One pass per snippet: reproduce, hypothesise, localise. Budget: 6 minutes each, in that order.
- A bug is a claim about your mental model; the failing input is the experiment.
- The fix should be smaller than the explanation of the bug.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Which hypothesis did you rank first, and what evidence moved it down the list?"**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Advanced (functions, dictionaries, and algorithmic reasoning assumed).
- Topics: debugging, regression.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a defensible plan; a complete solution may take
  longer and that is expected - state where you would resume.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: identify the pattern the worked instance uses before
  computing anything.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: Write the regression test that would have caught the defect earliest.
