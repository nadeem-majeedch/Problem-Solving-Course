## Problem

One billionaire joins a 9-person seminar: the mean salary explodes. Mean vs median, when each is honest, and how to state them.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, mean and median computed and contrasted on the worked instance, and one sentence on who benefits from reporting only the mean. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** salaries 9 x 30k + 1 x 5,000,000
- **Output:** mean 527k (honest but useless here); median 30k; report both or median only, with the distribution

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- One extreme point drags the mean; the median ignores magnitude. Neither is 'wrong' - the choice must be stated.
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Propose one extra rule that would change the verdict — is it an improvement?"**
