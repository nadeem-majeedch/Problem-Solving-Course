## Problem

Monthly subscriber counts in and out. Define churn rate precisely (what's the base?), compute, and explain a month that 'looks bad but isn't'.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, churn defined precisely (base, window, unit), and one wording choice that changes the reported number. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** jan: 1000 start, 50 join, 40 leave, end 1010; feb: 1010 start, 60 leave
- **Output:** jan churn 40/1000 = 4.0% (base = starting subscribers); feb 60/1010 ~ 5.9% - define the base first

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- 'Churn' is meaningless until you name the denominator: start-of-month, average, or end - each gives a different rate.
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.
