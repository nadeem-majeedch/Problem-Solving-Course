## Problem

Choose topics to study under an hour budget, maximising expected marks (weights and values). Knapsack DP; the table as the solution, not just the number.

Your deliverable is the answer for the worked instance, the DP table (item by budget) with one row and one column explained, and which choices the traceback recovers. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** hours [3, 4, 2], marks [50, 70, 40], budget 6
- **Output:** best: topic2(4h,70) + topic3(2h,40) = 110 marks (vs topic1+3 = 90; topic1+2 over budget)

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Classic knapsack: dp[h] = best marks with h hours. Fill row by row, each topic used at most once.
- Trust the recursive call: if f(smaller) is correct, the transition is all you must get right.
- A DP table is the call tree, cached.
