## Problem

'Sales up 50% then down 40%' — why that's a net gain, not a loss. Percentage bases; then reconstruct a table from percentage claims.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, the percentage recomputed correctly with its base named, and one rewording that removes the ambiguity. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** sales 100 -> up 50% -> 150 -> down 40% -> 90; two claims 'up 50, down 40'
- **Output:** net change 100 -> 90 = -10%; bases differ (150 for the fall) - percentages never add across different bases

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Each percentage has its own base. Track the absolute values and the mystery disappears.
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Which assumption would you attack first if you had to argue the other side?"**
