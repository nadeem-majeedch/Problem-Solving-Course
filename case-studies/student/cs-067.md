## Problem

A list is two sorted runs concatenated. Find the smallest element. Shows how preconditions (knowing structure) change the algorithm.

Your deliverable is the answer for the worked instance, the precondition stated, and the not-found behaviour demonstrated. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** [7, 9, 11, 2, 4, 6] (two sorted runs); minimum is 2
- **Output:** 2 at the run boundary; a plain min() scan works always - the structure allows better

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- First try: scan for the position where a[i] > a[i+1]; the next element is the minimum (or position 0).
- Sorted data is not just faster — it enables *different* algorithms.
- A scan is only as good as its stated precondition.
