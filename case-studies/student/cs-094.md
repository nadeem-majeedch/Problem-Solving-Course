## Problem

Fewest corridors between two rooms. Breadth-first search on a small grid/campus map; levels as distances.

Your deliverable is the graph model (nodes/edges), the traversal result for the worked instance, and the unreachable-case behaviour. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** grid campus: S at (0,0), T at (2,2); corridors right/down/up; wall at (1,1)
- **Output:** shortest walk 4 steps (e.g. down, right, down, right) - BFS levels are distances

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- BFS from S; the first time you pop T, its level is the distance. Walls are just missing edges.
- The modelling choice (what is a node?) is the actual problem.
- BFS layers are distances — that is why it answers 'fewest steps'.
