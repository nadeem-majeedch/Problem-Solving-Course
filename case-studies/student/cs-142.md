## Problem

Eleven committee members each wrote one name on a slip:

```
Zoe   Amir  Zoe   Bea   Amir  Cal   Zoe
Bea   Cal   Amir  Zoe
```

Your deliverables:

1. The tally per candidate.
2. The winner (most slips) and the runner-up.
3. The share of slips for the top two, as percentages of the valid
   ballots, to one decimal place.
4. The one-sentence announcement to the committee that reports the
   result **without** over-claiming (what does 11 slips NOT tell you?).

If the problem is ambiguous anywhere, state the assumption you are making
and carry on — a stated assumption is part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** the eleven slips
- **Output:** Zoe 4, Amir 3, Bea 2, Cal 2 — winner Zoe 36.4%,
  runner-up Amir 27.3% — one honest sentence

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Counting is easy; **not over-claiming** is the skill. Eleven slips
  cannot say what the twelfth member or the absentees would have done.
- Percentages must name their base: 4 of 11 valid ballots is not "40%".
- A winner under this rule is defined by the rule (most slips); had the
  rule required a majority (>50%), the same slips give a different
  outcome — the rule is part of the result.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"The committee chair asks you to announce 'Zoe wins by a
landslide.' Redraft the sentence so it stays true with the same
numbers."**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Beginner (no prior experience assumed).
- Topics: aggregation, communication, data.
- All eleven slips are valid; none is blank or double-named.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a first attempt; partial answers with
  clear reasoning are worth more than silent struggling.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: count in one pass, keeping one running number per name.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: the chair proposes a two-round runoff between the
  top two, with Bea's and Cal's slips redistributed by each slip's
  second choice — explain what information the ballots lack for that,
  and why.
