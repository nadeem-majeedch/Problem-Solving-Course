## Problem

The university shuttle poster says: **"a bus every ten minutes."** The
operational facts:

- Departures from the depot are scheduled at :00, :10, :20, :30, :40, :50.
- One loop — depot → stop A → stop B → stop C → depot — takes exactly
  25 minutes (legs: 4, 6, 7, 8).
- The fleet is **two buses**. A bus departs only when it is back at the
  depot; a bus that returns late departs late.

Your deliverables:

1. The actual depot departure times for the first six scheduled slots.
2. The **gap** between consecutive departures for those slots.
3. The verdict: does "a bus every ten minutes" hold, and if not, exactly
   which gaps break it?
4. The two-sentence notice to students that is honest — no cheerleading,
   no excuses.

If the problem is ambiguous anywhere, state the assumption you are making
and carry on — a stated assumption is part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** the schedule, loop legs, fleet size
- **Output:** `departures 0, 10, 25, 35, 50, 60` — gaps `10, 15, 10, 15,
  10` — verdict + notice

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Departure slots are a *wish*; the fleet is the *constraint*. The
  actual departure is `max(scheduled, back-at-depot time)`.
- Once one departure slips, every later gap inherits the slip — lateness
  propagates through the schedule like a carried error.
- The fix is arithmetic, not driving faster: a loop of 25 minutes with
  headway 10 needs a fleet of at least ⌈25/10⌉ = 3.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Operations proposes shortening the loop by three minutes
instead of buying a third bus. Does the poster claim become true?
Show the departure ledger that decides it."**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Foundational (multi-step schedule reasoning).
- Topics: scheduling, communication, assumptions.
- Legs are exact (no traffic variance) — this is a *timetable* claim,
  checked arithmetically.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a first attempt; partial answers with
  clear reasoning are worth more than silent struggling.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: write the return time of the bus that left at :00 before
  worrying about any other slot.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: with a three-bus fleet, find the loop length at
  which the ten-minute promise breaks *even at the depot*.
