## Problem

The exams office exports this file, `marks.csv`, exactly as written:

```
name,mark
Ayla,72
Ben,
Cemre,abs
Deniz,64
Elif,999
Ferid,81
```

The office's written policy says: a blank or `abs` means the student was
absent; a mark of `999` is a sentinel meaning *recheck with the invigilator*
and is not a real mark. Your deliverable has three parts:

1. Classify each of the six rows as `include`, `absent`, or `recheck`.
2. Count each class.
3. Compute the average of the included marks, to one decimal place — and
   state one thing that would go wrong if the `999` row were averaged in
   by a program that only checked "is it a number?".

If the problem is ambiguous anywhere, state the assumption you are making
and carry on — a stated assumption is part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** the six-row table above, the office policy as given
- **Output:** `included 3, absent 2, recheck 1, mean 72.3`

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- "Blank", `abs`, and `999` are three *different* kinds of missingness;
  a program that treats "not a normal mark" as one bucket loses the
  distinction the office policy cares about.
- The average is only defined **after** the classification — the order of
  the deliverable is the order of the reasoning.
- Validation is a decision, not a mechanical filter: each rule comes from
  a human policy, and your job is to apply it and name it.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"The office now sends a file where one mark is `72,0` — same
row, two values. Where in your classification does this break, and what
do you ask the office before touching the data?"**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Foundational (multi-step reasoning on a small table).
- Topics: validation, missing values, communication.
- The policy text is authoritative; if a policy seems odd, you apply it
  *and* flag it, you do not silently "fix" it.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a first attempt; partial answers with
  clear reasoning are worth more than silent struggling.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: how many *distinct* reasons can a mark be missing for?
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: suppose `999` could also be a genuine (bizarre)
  mark someone typed. What must change in the *data format* — not in
  your program — so the sentinel stays unambiguous?
