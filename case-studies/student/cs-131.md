## Problem

A campus help desk serves students one at a time. Eight students will
arrive today; their arrival minute and service duration (in minutes) are
known in advance:

```
student:  S1   S2   S3   S4   S5   S6   S7   S8
arrives:   0    1    2    6    7   10   12   13
service:   4    3    5    2    4    3    2    5
```

Rules: students are served in arrival order; a server starts a student at
`max(arrival, when the server next frees)`; nobody jumps the queue.

Compare two designs:

- **One desk:** a single server, as today.
- **Two desks:** a waiting student takes any free desk; if both are free,
  desk 1; if both are busy, they wait for the one that frees first.

Your deliverables:

1. For each design, each student's **waiting time** (start − arrival).
2. The **total waiting time** and the **makespan** (last finish) per design.
3. One sentence on the trade-off: the second desk costs money — under
   what conditions is it worth it?

If the problem is ambiguous anywhere, state the assumption you are making
and carry on — a stated assumption is part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** the arrival/service table, the two designs
- **Output:** one desk: total wait 48 min, last finish 28 — two desks:
  total wait 3 min, last finish 18

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- This is a **trace**, not a stochastic simulation: every number is
  determined, so two correct answers must agree exactly.
- The state you must carry is tiny — "when does each desk free up" — and
  the whole simulation is one update rule applied in order.
- Total waiting and makespan can move in different proportions; a design
  decision should say which one it is buying.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Arrivals double next semester (same gaps, twice the service
load spread over more students). Does the two-desk advantage grow,
shrink, or stay put — and how would you check without simulating all
day?"**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Intermediate (multi-step simulation with a design decision).
- Topics: simulation, service systems, trade-offs.
- Service is non-preemptive: a desk finishes the student it started.
- Classroom-scale inputs unless the problem says otherwise.
- Time-box: about five minutes for a first attempt; partial answers with
  clear reasoning are worth more than silent struggling.
- Optional stretch (not required): describe an alternative approach and
  say when it beats the obvious one.

## Hints and extension

- First nudge: for one desk there is a single running "free at" time —
  find it for S1, S2, S3 and watch the pattern.
- If stuck after three minutes, write the input/output line you expect and
  trace the smallest example by hand.
- After the reveal, attempt the extension below before the next lecture.
- Extension challenge: with two desks and *both* free at S3's arrival,
  the rule says desk 1. Invent an instance where that tie rule changes
  the makespan, and one where it does not.
