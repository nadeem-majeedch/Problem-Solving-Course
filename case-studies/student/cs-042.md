## Problem

Remove duplicates three ways: preserving order with a seen-set, via sorting, and the naive quadratic way. Compare and test all three.

Your deliverable is the three deduplication strategies applied to the worked instance, a one-line cost statement for each, and one input where the sorted approach visibly changes the result. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** [3, 1, 3, 2, 1, 3]
- **Output:** order-keeping: [3, 1, 2]; sort-based: [1, 2, 3]; naive O(n^2): same as order-keeping

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Implement all three, then time them on 10k elements - the point is the comparison, not the answer.
- Slices are half-open: they make boundaries explicit instead of hidden in loop conditions.
- Aliasing is the difference between copying a list and copying a name.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"When is the extra memory of slicing worth it, and when would you insist on in-place?"**
