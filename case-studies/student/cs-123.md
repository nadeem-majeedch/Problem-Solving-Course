## Problem

Insurance costs more than expected loss. Why rational people still buy: variance, ruin, and utility in plain language. EV as a tool, not a god.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, the expected value computed for the worked instance, the ruin-aversion caveat stated, and one contested case. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** phone worth 600; loss chance 10%/year; premium 80
- **Output:** EV loss = 60 << 80 premium: 'bad deal' by EV; still rational for ruin-aversion - EV is a tool, not the verdict

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- EV says decline; utility says the 600 loss hurts more than linearly. The case is about knowing which model fits.
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Propose one extra rule that would change the verdict — is it an improvement?"**
