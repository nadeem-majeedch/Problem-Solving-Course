## Problem

Greedy coin change works for one coin system and fails for another. Find the failing case, explain why, and state when greedy is safe.

Your deliverable is the greedy choice justified with an exchange argument, the counterexample attempt that fails to break it, and the answer for the worked instance. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** coins {1, 5, 10, 25} for 87 -> greedy 25x3+10x1+1x2 = 6 coins; system {1, 3, 4} for 6 -> greedy 4+1+1 = 3, optimal 3+3 = 2
- **Output:** greedy optimal for the first system; FAILS for the second - one counterexample kills the claim

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Greedy is a hypothesis, not a theorem. Hunt for the smallest counterexample before trusting it.
- Trust the recursive call: if f(smaller) is correct, the transition is all you must get right.
- A DP table is the call tree, cached.
