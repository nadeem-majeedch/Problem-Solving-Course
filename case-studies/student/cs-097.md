## Problem

From a queue-length log, answer: busiest hour, average wait proxy, quietest day. Framing questions before computing.

Your deliverable is the stated assumptions, the service rule made precise (numerator, denominator, unit), and one cohort split that changes the story. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** queue lengths by hour: 11:00->8, 12:00->22, 13:00->25, 14:00->6; week total 950 served
- **Output:** busiest hour 13:00; 'average wait proxy' must be defined (e.g. queue/time) before computing; quietest day stated

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- Write the three questions first, then the exact definition of each measure - the computing is trivial afterwards.
- Writing the rules IS the solution; code is transcription.
- Every assumption is a place where two good answers can differ.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"Propose one extra rule that would change the verdict — is it an improvement?"**

## Constraints and assumptions

- Difficulty: Beginner (no prior experience assumed).
- Topics: data, aggregation.
