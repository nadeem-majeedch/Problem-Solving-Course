## Problem

Find all courses taught in a room with capacity at least X and free on Tuesdays. Filtering as a search with a compound predicate.

Your deliverable is the answer for the worked instance, the precondition stated, and the not-found behaviour demonstrated. If the problem is ambiguous anywhere,
state the assumption you are making and carry on — a stated assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Examples

- **Input:** courses [(CS1, room 40, cap 30, Tue), (DS1, room 12, cap 60, Wed), (AI, room 41, cap 45, Tue)], cap >= 40, free Tue
- **Output:** [DS1? no - Wed] -> matches: AI only (room cap 45, Tue); DS1 has cap but wrong day

Solve for the worked instance first. Then ask what would change if one
number, name, or condition were replaced by something stranger.

## What to notice

- A compound predicate: capacity AND day. Filter = search that returns ALL matches.
- Sorted data is not just faster — it enables *different* algorithms.
- A scan is only as good as its stated precondition.

## Discussion starter

After the solution is revealed, the class spends five minutes on this
prompt: **"If this scan ran 10,000 times a day, what would you precompute?"**
